# Хендлы: @XOpenSource (анонс).

X now publishes a transparency report on how it limits your reach. We read ours the same week — clean.

Under the Hood lists the exact labels X applied to your posts and account last month, with their effects: a warning shown to non-followers, and like, reply, and repost blocked for them. The new part @XOpenSource shipped yesterday: it now names the specific posts, not just counts — if fewer than 5% of your posts carry a label.

Read the fine print, because the report is honest about being retrospective. It covers the last complete month and lands about ten days later. A label stays "under observation" for seven days before it is final. Access requires an account older than a year with ten posts in the report month.

That cadence changes how to use it. The report is an audit trail, not an alarm. When impressions drop without explanation, the direct check cannot warn you the same day — the report confirms what happened last month, a week after you noticed. So indirect checks stay your early warning: does search return your posts, do impressions fall without cause. The report is where you go after, with names and dates, to argue from facts instead of vibes.

We ran the audit for our account: no labels on posts, none on the account. Our indirect checks — search visibility, impression tracking — had said the same. Now both layers agree, and the direct one is on the record.

What to steal:

- Run the monthly report as an audit, not a monitor: x.com/i/under_the_hood, download the JSON, archive it
- Keep indirect checks as the daily layer — the report is retrospective by design
- If a label appears: it names the posts, the effect, and stays under observation for seven days — that is your appeal window

The interesting part is not that X hides reach. It is that the hiding is now documented, listed, and downloadable — which turns "the algorithm is suppressing me" from a feeling into a claim you can check.
