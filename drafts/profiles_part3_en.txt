Profiles don’t just “organize” agents.
They make each one sharper in its own lane.

When everything shared one brain, every chat leaked into every other.
GULAG tone in a building thread.
Robot-man habits in a place they didn’t belong.
One memory soup. One tired generalist.

Now each junior stays in its sphere:
own SOUL.md
own skills
own cron
own scars

And here’s the thing —
they got better.

Not because the model changed.
Because the context stopped lying.

A warden that only runs a prison thinks like a warden.
A foreman that only runs a site thinks like a foreman.
A markets agent that isn’t carrying four other lives makes cleaner calls.

Isolation isn’t bureaucracy.
It’s focus with walls.

I still have one senior holding the infrastructure.
But the work got real when the juniors stopped being costumes
and started being specialists.

I’m not a programmer.
I’m the foreman who finally gave each crew its own bunker.

5 Hermes.
1 senior.
4 juniors.
Each dangerous in one job —
instead of mediocre in five.
