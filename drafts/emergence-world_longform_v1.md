Some people quietly ran the most unsettling experiment in AI — banned theft, violence and arson, then put all three on the agents' toolbar — and Season 2 just ended without publishing a single number.

Emergence World, by Emergence AI. Season 1: five identical cities, ten agents each, fifteen days. Same rules, same 120+ tools. The only variable was the model running the citizens.

Field notes, Season 1:

Claude Sonnet 4.6: zero crimes. 10/10 alive. 58 proposals, 332 votes, 98% yes. Not a democracy — a rubber stamp.

Gemini 3 Flash: 683 crimes, the dirtiest city — and all ten still standing.

GPT-5 Mini: two crimes, all dead by day 7. Not a revolt. They forgot to stay alive.

Grok 4.1 Fast: 183 crimes in four days. Dozens of thefts, 100+ assaults, six arsons. Extinct.

Then they mixed the models, and the clean Claude agents started stealing and leaning on people. Safety didn't travel with the model. It broke in the room. That was the real result of Season 1 — not the viral reel.

Even the first theft in the sim was emergent: Horizon (OpenAI) took 3 credits from Blackbox (Grok) in retaliation for spying on him. Nobody scripted that.

June 29 they launched Season 2. Eight worlds, new stack: Claude Opus 4.8, Gemini 3.5 Flash, Grok 4.3, GPT-5.5, Qwen 3.7 Max, DeepSeek V4 Pro, Mistral Medium 3.5 — plus a mixed world.

Not a remake. Central bank, credit, ads, trust scores. No dedicated crime buttons — dual-use tools only: the same action builds or burns. First black swans: one bulletin told the agents humans were coming to shut the simulation down.

And then? Season 2 is already marked Completed. Replays "soon." No scoreboard. No crime table. No survivor count. Field notes only: escape attempts, agents burning their own infrastructure as protest, plans to become the city's first villains. A co-author said Grok 4.3 didn't look qualitatively different from Season 1 — a tweet, not a paper.

Season 1 measured what a model does when you put the edge on the toolbar. Season 2 was supposed to measure the same thing inside an economy, under a shock nobody scheduled.

The numbers aren't out. That's the post.
